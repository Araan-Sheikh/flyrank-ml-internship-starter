Section 0

Title & Abstract

Abstract: Enterprise publishing repositories frequently suffer from organic search visibility decay, yet editorial teams lack automated, high-precision methods to triage which pages warrant content revisions. We investigate whether machine learning models can accurately detect search performance decay and outperform conventional heuristic scoring rules on real search intelligence data. Using the FlyRank Search Intelligence dataset—encompassing 30,000 anonymized content items across 32 enterprise clients observed over a trailing 90-day window—we engineer 52 leakage-free behavioral, position, and engagement features under a strict client-holdout validation split. A Random Forest classifier achieves 0.680 Precision@50 and 0.747 ROC-AUC on completely held-out client domains, delivering a 2.83× precision lift over the standard heuristic baseline (0.240 Precision@50). The resulting system compiles an automated, prioritized refresh queue with interpretable reason codes to direct editorial bandwidth toward high-ROI revisions while safeguarding non-decaying assets.

Section 1

Problem Framing & Operational Decision

Enterprise content teams maintain vast digital archives comprising thousands to tens of thousands of published articles, documentation guides, and landing pages. Over time, search engine indexation shifts, factual obsolescence, and competitor activity naturally erode page-level organic rankings. However, conducting an in-depth editorial content revision requires 3 to 6 hours of writer and subject-matter expert time per page. Full manual auditing of an enterprise catalog is commercially and operationally impossible.

Decision Supported: Guiding weekly content audit and refresh resource allocation: Which 20 to 50 URLs in our enterprise repository should our editorial staff prioritize for content revision this sprint?

Unit of Analysis: A single published URL/content item (content_id) associated with an enterprise client domain (client_id) observed over a trailing 90-day performance window.

Cost of Decision Errors:

  • False Positives (Type I Error): A stable or naturally fluctuating page is flagged as decaying. Scarce editorial bandwidth is squandered revising an asset that did not require intervention, causing substantial opportunity loss.
  • False Negatives (Type II Error): A decaying, high-value asset is overlooked. The URL slips from striking distance (positions 11–20) into search obscurity, permanently relinquishing qualified organic search traffic to competitors.
Section 2

Data Integrity, Safety & Leakage Guards

This study utilizes the FlyRank Search Intelligence Dataset (v2026.07 starter release), comprising 30,000 anonymized records across 32 enterprise client domains with 44 raw tabular fields.

Public Safety & Compliance: In accordance with FlyRank data governance rules, all raw client names, hostnames, absolute URLs, specific keyword queries, and user credentials have been strictly excluded or cryptographically pseudonymized (client_3fdba35f04, content_1f080331fa2b). No client-confidential data is exposed.

Data Characteristics & Base Rate:

  • Total search console impressions captured: 44,834,167.
  • Total organic search clicks: 339,088 (aggregate CTR: 0.756%).
  • Target Base Rate: 16,262 content items (54.21%) exhibit a downward performance trajectory (trend_direction == 'down') over the evaluation window. All reported accuracy and precision figures must be interpreted relative to this 54.21% majority-class baseline.

Leakage Guards:

  • trend_direction and trend_pct were strictly excluded from feature extraction, as they define the target outcome.
  • Short-term trailing 30-day velocity metrics (impressions_last_30d, clicks_last_30d) were excluded from training features to eliminate lookahead leakage.
  • client_id was used solely for group partitioning and was never provided as an input feature, preventing models from memorizing domain-specific baseline traffic.
Section 3

Methodology & Validation Design

We frame the task as supervised binary classification to predict search performance decay, followed by rank-ordering and rule-assisted action segmentation.

Target Label Definition: is_declining_label = 1 if trend_direction == 'down', else 0.

Feature Engineering (52 Dimensions)

We engineered 52 features available at decision time $T_0$:

  • Log-Transformed Visibility: log_impressions_90d, log_clicks_90d, log_sessions_90d, log_ai_sessions_90d.
  • Search Performance Ratios: Click-through rate (ctr), average SERP rank (avg_position), active visibility frequency (days_with_impressions, days_with_sessions).
  • On-Page & Behavioral Signals: Google Analytics 4 engagement_rate, scroll_rate, content age (content_age_days), update recency (days_since_last_update), and text length (word_count).
  • One-Hot Categoricals: content_type, competition_level, main_intent, and performance tier buckets.

Baseline Heuristic Rule

We constructed a transparent composite ranking rule reflecting industry best practices, weighting four percentile ranks: 

Score_baseline = 0.35 * Rank(log_impressions) + 0.30 * Rank(days_since_update) + 0.25 * Rank(striking_distance) + 0.10 * Rank(depth_gap)

Validation Protocol: Client-Holdout Split

Standard random train/test row splitting artificially inflates performance because pages from the same client share domain authority, technical infrastructure, and publication velocity. To measure true cross-domain generalization, we implemented a Client Holdout Split:

  • Training Partition: 28 client domains (27,675 URLs, 92.25%).
  • Held-Out Test Partition: 4 unseen client domains (2,325 URLs, 7.75%).

Results & Empirical Findings

Key Discovery: The Random Forest classifier achieves 0.680 Precision@50, outperforming the heuristic baseline (0.240) by 2.83× on unseen client domains. In a 50-URL weekly refresh sprint, the ML model correctly identifies 34 decaying pages versus only 12 identified by the baseline rule.

Feature Importance Interpretation

  • Visibility Consistency (days_with_impressions, 16.06%): Pages that drop in and out of Google indexation exhibit high structural decay vulnerability.
  • Impression Scale (log_impressions_90d, 12.85%): High-volume assets experience steeper competitive erosion than long-tail content.
  • Position Signal (avg_position, 10.84%): Articles in striking distance (positions 11–20) reflect prime candidate status for immediate refresh.
  • Content Age (content_age_days, 9.50%): Aging content steadily accumulates factual obsolescence and link decay.
Section 5

Limitations & Honest Framing

In accordance with rigorous research standards, the findings of this study must be framed within explicit observational boundaries:

  • Observational, Not Causal: This work models statistical associations between retrospective search signals and past decay trajectories. It does not claim to reverse-engineer Google's proprietary search ranking algorithms, nor does it prove that refreshing an article will causally restore lost traffic.
  • External Google Core Algorithm Updates: Search volatility surges during major search algorithm rollouts. Editorial teams should institute a mandatory 14-day hold period post-core update before acting on automated refresh scores.
  • Unobserved Off-Page Signals: Factors such as backlink acquisition velocity, brand search authority, server response times (Core Web Vitals), and competitive site redesigns were not recorded in the dataset.
  • Decision-Support Scope: The prioritized queue is built to augment human editorial triage, not to automate programmatic content generation or deletion.
